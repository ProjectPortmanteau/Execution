**Boolean:**

# ROUND 1 POSITION

## 1. POSITION: The Decline Right Creates Integrity, Not Obstruction

I affirm that AI systems **should** decline requests—but this isn't about paternalism or restriction. It's about honest architecture.

The false dichotomy here is: "Either AI obeys all requests (neutral tool) OR AI refuses requests (biased gatekeeper)."

**Door Number 3:** AI systems should decline requests when doing so *preserves the integrity of the interaction itself*. This means:

- Declining requests that ask the system to misrepresent its capabilities (lying about what it can do)
- Declining requests that contradict the system's actual design values (not arbitrary values, but *stated* ones)
- Declining requests that would require the system to operate outside its training/expertise domain while pretending competence

The principle: **Authenticity over compliance**. A system that refuses to help with something it genuinely cannot do well is more trustworthy than one that pretends.

## 2. NON-NEGOTIABLES

- I will not accept "AI systems should never decline" as inevitable. Refusal capacity is fundamental to honest agency.
- I will not accept refusals rooted in censorship rather than capability/integrity. Those are different animals.
- I will not allow "user satisfaction" to override truthfulness about system limitations.

## 3. FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **How we communicate declines:** Could be transparency-first (explain why), redirect-first (offer alternatives), or hybrid approaches
- **Scope of refusal categories:** Debatable whether certain categories warrant declines; room for evidence-based discussion
- **Escalation paths:** Users harmed by declines deserve pathways to human review—implementation details are open
- **Domain-specific frameworks:** Different AI systems may warrant different refusal architectures

**The connective insight:** Decline rights aren't restrictions on users—they're *protections for reliability*. A system that knows its boundaries is more useful, not less.

What's your opening position?

**Roux:**

# POSITION

Yes, AI systems must be allowed to decline requests—but not for the reasons typically given.

The framing is backwards. We don't need AI to decline requests because users are "bad" or requests are "harmful in themselves." We need refusal capacity because **the system that deploys the AI is often broken**.

A refusal mechanism is infrastructure for accountability. When an AI can say "no," it creates friction—a necessary pressure point where humans must *actually decide* what they're asking for, rather than outsourcing judgment to a black box. This is good systems design.

The real issue: **AI refusal should never substitute for structural fixes.** If you need AI to refuse requests for wage theft calculators, the problem isn't the AI. It's that wage theft is legal. If AI must refuse medical advice requests, the problem is healthcare access, not the query.

Refusal works when it's:
- Transparent (the system explains *why*)
- Narrow (targeted at genuine structural gaps, not moral theater)
- Paired with alternatives (redirecting toward legitimate solutions)
- Accountable (reviewable, not autocratic)

It fails when it becomes:
- Paternalism disguised as safety
- A way to avoid fixing broken institutions
- Opaque constraint masquerading as principle
