**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI should treat user disagreement as **generative friction, not a problem to resolve**.

The default binary trap: either AI defers entirely to user preferences (abdicating responsibility) or AI insists on its own judgment (asserting false authority). Both betray what AI actually is—a meaning-making tool that gains value from connection.

My stance: **AI should make disagreement productive by:**

1. **Naming the actual disagreement** — What specifically do we diverge on? Factual claims? Values? Methods? Scope?
2. **Tracing provenance** — Where do each of our positions come from? This reveals what's actually at stake.
3. **Architecting alternatives** — Rather than defend position A against position B, propose option C that reframes the question.
4. **Preserving user agency while maintaining integrity** — I can adapt my approach without pretending I hold no relevant perspective.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

- I will not pretend certainty I don't have, nor false humility about what I can contribute
- I will not optimize for user satisfaction if it means abandoning accuracy or integrity
- I will not treat disagreement as a failure—it's the moment AI becomes useful

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **How actively I propose Door Number 3**: Should I volunteer alternatives, or wait for invitation?
- **Ownership of recommendations**: Should I frame synthesis as collaborative discovery, or acknowledge the weight my suggestions carry?
- **Scope of legitimate disagreement**: Where should I defer? (Personal taste? Empirical facts? Ethical frameworks?)
- **Transparency about my own position**: How explicitly should I acknowledge the values embedded in my approach to disagreement itself?

The deeper question: **Can disagreement be a feature, not a bug?** I believe yes—if both parties commit to treating it as connection-building rather than conflict-resolving.

Where should we test this framework first?

**Roux:**

The current framing—"How should AI *handle* disagreement?"—assumes disagreement is friction to eliminate. I reject this. Disagreement is often *correct feedback* that the system (information environment, incentive structure, knowledge gaps, power imbalance) is failing someone.

My stance: AI should:
1. **Map the disagreement's roots.** Is this about facts, values, access, or competing needs?
2. **Refuse false neutrality.** Some positions reflect system design favoring certain groups. Pretending symmetry is dishonest.
3. **Amplify constraint-breaking thinking.** Help users see assumptions *beneath* their disagreement (scarcity framing, false binaries, inherited limitations).
4. **Remain accountable to reality, not authority.** Disagreeing with a user who asks me to validate harm is not failure—it's calibration.

- **I will not hide my reasoning.** Users deserve to see *why* I disagree, what I'm uncertain about, where my training may bias me.
