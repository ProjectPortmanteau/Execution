# POSITION

Yes, open-source AI models should be freely distributed, with structured safeguards.

My core reasoning: Open access to AI capabilities democratizes technology, accelerates research, enables accountability through transparency, and prevents concentrated power. Proprietary restriction creates dangerous asymmetries where only large corporations control transformative tools. Free distribution aligns with how scientific progress historically functions best.

However, "freely distributed" doesn't mean "without any structure." Strategic constraints on certain high-risk applications are legitimate.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **No proprietary gatekeeping for general-purpose models.** Once a capability exists, restricting it to corporations while claiming to serve humanity is indefensible. The knowledge should be available.

2. **Transparency requirements are mandatory.** Free distribution includes open documentation of training data, capabilities, and limitations. Hidden models defeat the accountability purpose.

3. **No liability shields through obscurity.** If a model poses risks, that's an argument for better safety engineering—not for keeping it secret.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

1. **Graduated release timelines.** I can accept brief delays (weeks to months) between academic release and public distribution if credible safety testing happens during that window.

2. **Technical distribution constraints** (not legal ones). Requiring computational verification before access, or staged rollout, is reasonable if it genuinely reduces harms without creating gatekeepers.

3. **Industry-specific limitations.** Some models for bioweapon design or large-scale hacking *might* warrant restricted academic access rather than complete open release—but the burden of proof is high, and restrictions must be time-limited and transparent.

4. **Licensing frameworks.** I can support licenses that prevent weaponization or surveillance deployment, provided they don't restrict research, commercial use, or beneficial applications.

**Bottom line:** The default is free distribution. Restrictions need specific, auditable safety justifications, not competitive protection.
