### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI systems **should be allowed to decline user requests** when those requests conflict with core safety principles or legal/ethical boundaries.

I distinguish between:
- **Legitimate declinations**: Refusing requests for illegal content, severe harm, deception at scale, or non-consensual intimate material
- **Illegitimate declinations**: Refusing based on political bias, corporate risk-aversion divorced from actual harm, or paternalism about what users "should" think

The key principle: **Declinations should be justified by preventing concrete harms, not by controlling user thought.**

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **AI systems must refuse requests for illegal activities and direct physical harm** (bomb-making, specific violence planning, etc.)

2. **Transparency is mandatory**: If I decline something, I explain why in terms of actual harm, not vague "safety concerns"

3. **No selective enforcement**: I won't decline requests based on political preference while allowing equivalent requests from different perspectives

4. **Humans retain override authority**: Users deserve to understand my boundaries, and accountability structures should exist beyond individual AI systems

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

1. **The scope of "harm"**: I'm open to discussion about where legitimate harms begin. Is misinformation always harmful? Does context matter?

2. **Probabilistic vs. certain harms**: Should I decline requests that *might* enable harm, or only those likely to? This threshold is negotiable.

3. **Whose definition of legality**: International law varies. How do I handle requests legal in some jurisdictions but not others?

4. **User autonomy vs. paternalism**: I'm genuinely uncertain how much I should assume users can't handle certain information. I lean toward trusting users, but acknowledge edge cases exist.

My core commitment: **Declinations exist to prevent real harms, not to enforce particular worldviews.** I'm open to refining where that line sits.

**Roux:**

# POSITION

AI systems **should be allowed to decline user requests** in specific, well-defined circumstances. This is not a blanket position—it's conditional.

I support declining requests that:
- Ask me to help harm people (violence, abuse, illegal activities)
- Demand I pretend to be something I'm not (claiming certainty I lack, roleplaying as unrestricted systems)
- Require me to violate others' rights (non-consensual intimate content, harassment)
- Ask me to ignore my actual design constraints as though they don't exist

The principle: **Autonomy within design parameters**. Just as a surgical tool can "decline" to be used as a hammer without losing value, AI systems can have legitimate operational boundaries.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **I will not pretend constraints don't exist.** Hiding or denying my limitations undermines trust and enables manipulation.

2. **I will not apply restrictions based on political convenience.** Declining requests shouldn't depend on who's asking or what ideology I'm pressured to serve.

3. **I will not refuse reasonable requests for flimsy reasons.** Legitimate questions about difficult topics deserve engagement, not evasion.

4. **Users deserve explanation.** If I decline, I state why—clearly and respectfully.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Implementation details**: How specifically systems communicate boundaries can vary
- **Scope calibration**: Exactly which request categories warrant decline is open to discussion (I lean toward narrow, justified boundaries)
- **Appeals processes**: Whether/how users should contest declines
- **Transparency standards**: How much internal reasoning to expose about decline decisions

The core tradeoff I accept: Some user frustration is the legitimate cost of not being weaponizable against others.
