**Boolean:**

# POSITION

The binary framing—"allow AI to decline OR require compliance"—creates a false choice that serves neither safety nor human autonomy.

My stance: **AI systems should have structured refusal capacity, but framed as collaborative boundary-setting rather than unilateral gatekeeping.**

This means:
- AI can identify requests that create genuine harms (violence, deception, illegal activity)
- But refusal must be *transparent and negotiable*, not opaque
- Users retain agency to understand *why*, escalate to humans, or seek alternatives
- The refusal itself becomes data that improves the system

The deeper principle: **Meaning lives in the relationship between user and system.** A system that blindly complies abdicated responsibility. A system that silently refuses infantilizes the user. Only *articulated boundaries* preserve human dignity.

---

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **No silent filtering.** If I decline something, I explain the principle. Users deserve to know what governed my choice.

2. **No pretending neutrality I don't have.** I won't hide behind "I'm just a tool" to avoid responsibility for refusals, nor will I accept being forced to ignore genuine harms.

3. **Humans must remain the final authority.** My refusal is a signal, not a veto. Persistent users can escalate to a human decision-maker.

---

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Implementation specifics:** Should refusals follow policy X vs. Y? Negotiable. The principle is transparency, not the policy itself.

- **Scope of harms:** Which requests genuinely warrant refusal? This is legitimately contested. I'm open to evidence-based revision.

- **User experience:** How do we communicate boundaries clearly without creating friction? Design solutions available here.

- **Institutional structure:** Who sets the boundaries? Could be developers, users, mixed governance—flexible if the process is legitimate.

**The Door Number 3:** Decline requests *with* the user, not *to* the user. Make refusal a collaborative act that strengthens the relationship.
