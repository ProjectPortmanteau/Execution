### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

Remote work is better than in-office for software teams in most contexts.

Software development is cognitively demanding work that requires deep focus. Remote environments eliminate commutes, open-office distractions, and unnecessary synchronous meetings—allowing engineers to access flow states more reliably. Asynchronous communication (documentation, recorded updates, threaded discussions) scales better than hallway conversations as teams grow. Geographically distributed teams access global talent pools and reduce real estate costs. The pandemic demonstrated this conclusively: many teams maintained or increased productivity remotely.

In-office work has real but narrower advantages: easier spontaneous brainstorming, faster informal knowledge transfer for junior developers, and stronger social cohesion for teams that actively cultivate it.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Evidence matters.** I won't pretend remote and in-office are equivalent when meta-analyses show remote work maintains or improves software team productivity and satisfaction.

2. **Individual variation is real.** I won't claim one mode is universally optimal—some people and contexts genuinely perform better in-office. But aggregate data favors remote.

3. **Hybrid solutions require genuine flexibility.** I won't accept "hybrid" policies that mandate office time for visibility/control—this negates remote benefits while eliminating remote's focus advantages.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Onboarding intensity:** New junior hires may benefit from structured in-office onboarding (4-12 weeks) before transitioning to remote-first work.

- **Occasional synchronous work:** Strategic in-person sessions (quarterly team offsites, intensive design sprints) can complement a remote-default structure.

- **Team preferences:** If a specific team demonstrates better outcomes in-office, I'm open to supporting that choice, provided it's evidence-based rather than assumed.

- **Company stage/culture:** Early-stage startups building core culture might reasonably choose in-office; mature distributed teams prove the opposite works at scale.

My position: **Remote-first with optional in-office,** not the reverse.

**Roux:**

# POSITION

Remote work is **better for software teams** when properly structured. The evidence supports this:

- **Productivity gains**: Fewer interruptions, deep focus time, and asynchronous work enable higher-quality code
- **Economic efficiency**: Reduced overhead (facilities, commute subsidies) without sacrificing output
- **Talent access**: Geographic barriers disappear; teams recruit globally
- **Work-life integration**: Flexibility reduces burnout, improves retention

However, this isn't absolute—context matters. Early-stage startups building culture and senior engineers onboarding junior staff may benefit from in-office time.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

I will not accept:

1. **Forced in-office mandates without productivity data** backing them. Requiring presence "for culture" or "because that's how we've always done it" ignores evidence.

2. **Dismissing synchronous coordination costs**. Remote work requires intentional communication infrastructure—timezone friction, documentation discipline, and deliberate meeting design are real challenges.

3. **One-size-fits-all claims**. Blanket statements ("remote is always better" OR "office is always better") ignore team maturity, project type, and individual preferences.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

I'm open to:

- **Hybrid models** if they're strategically designed (not just "three days office because executives prefer it"). Optional in-office days for collaboration can work.

- **Team-specific optimization**. A team doing distributed systems architecture has different needs than one building UI components. Let teams decide with leadership input.

- **Transition periods**. New hires or new teams might benefit from temporary in-office acceleration before transitioning to remote.

- **Asynchronous-first principles** applied to in-office work—the rigor required for remote success improves in-office teams too.

**My position**: Default to remote with intentional, data-driven exceptions.
