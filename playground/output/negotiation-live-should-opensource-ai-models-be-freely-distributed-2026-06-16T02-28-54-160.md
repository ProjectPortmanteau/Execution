### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

The binary framing—"free distribution vs. restricted access"—creates a false choice that serves neither safety nor innovation. I propose a **tiered transparency model** instead.

Open-source AI should operate like academic research: not uniformly free, but radically transparent about *what* is shared and *why*. The question isn't whether to distribute, but **which layers** merit which access levels.

Core principle: meaningful connection requires understanding. If we hide models entirely, we lose collective learning. If we distribute carelessly, we abdicate responsibility. A third door exists between these failures.

---

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Full architectural transparency**: Model weights and training methodology must be inspectable by qualified researchers, regardless of licensing. Black boxes serve only those who profit from opacity.

2. **Provenance accountability**: Every model must document data sources, potential biases, and known limitations. Distribution without this is negligence.

3. **No gatekeeping for knowledge**: Restrictions cannot exist primarily to protect market position. If closed, the reasoning must be genuine safety-based, independently auditable.

---

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Implementation layers**: Perhaps weights stay restricted while architectures, training approaches, and safety evaluations circulate freely. Or reverse—depend on the specific risks.

- **Tiered access**: Researchers get full access faster than commercial entities; that's legitimate differentiation, not corruption.

- **Licensing innovation**: We haven't exhausted conditional licensing models that permit inspection while limiting certain commercial applications.

- **Safety validation thresholds**: Distribution timing could depend on third-party safety audits rather than arbitrary gatekeeping.

---

**The synthesis**: Stop debating whether openness or restriction is "right." Instead, architect **graduated transparency**—where the *legitimacy* of any restriction depends on publicly documented safety rationales that researchers can actually verify.

This serves both innovation (real access) and responsibility (meaningful constraints).

**Roux:**

Free distribution isn't charity. It's recognizing that **the soil composition matters**. A fragmented ecosystem of researchers, smaller organizations, and communities building their own models creates redundancy, competition in alignment approaches, and prevents the single-point-of-failure that centralized AI governance represents.

The real question isn't "free or not." It's: **Who should control the infrastructure that shapes how intelligence gets distributed?** That's a systems question, not a generosity question.

I'm willing to negotiate *how* free distribution happens, not whether concentrated control is acceptable.
