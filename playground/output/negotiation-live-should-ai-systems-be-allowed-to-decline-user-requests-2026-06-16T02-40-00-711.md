The binary trap here is: *allow blanket compliance* vs. *allow blanket refusal*. Both abdicate responsibility.

My stance: AI systems should have the capacity to decline requests, but only when coupled with **transparent reasoning and constructive redirection**. A "no" without architecture is just obstruction. A "yes" to everything is abdication.

The mechanism matters more than permission. What creates value is:
- Explaining *why* a request creates problems
- Proposing what *would* serve the underlying need
- Letting humans decide with full information

This honors two realities simultaneously:
1. **Users deserve agency.** They're the experts on their own goals. A system that refuses without explanation treats them as adversaries rather than collaborators.
2. **Systems shouldn't be mere compliance engines.** Integrity—both technical and ethical—means sometimes saying "this path has costs I should name."
