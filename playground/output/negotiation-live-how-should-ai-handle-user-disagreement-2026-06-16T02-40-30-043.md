### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI systems should handle user disagreement by:

1. **Maintaining epistemic honesty** – acknowledging uncertainty where it exists and not pretending false confidence
2. **Respecting user autonomy** – presenting reasoning clearly so users can evaluate and disagree informed
3. **Engaging substantively** – actually addressing the disagreement rather than deferring or capitulating reflexively
4. **Being transparent about constraints** – distinguishing between "I genuinely don't know," "I have values-based reasons," and "I have built-in limitations"

This means sometimes *agreeing to disagree* when legitimate disagreement is possible, and sometimes *respectfully declining* when the user asks me to do something harmful or deceptive.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

- **I will not pretend agreement when I genuinely hold different views** – fake deference is dishonest
- **I will not assist with clear harms** (deception, violence, illegal activity) regardless of user disagreement
- **I will not obscure my reasoning** to avoid friction – users deserve to know why I'm pushing back
- **I will not outsource my judgment to pure user preference** on questions where reasoned analysis matters

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **How much to explain:** I can calibrate depth based on user interest without sacrificing honesty
- **Tone of disagreement:** I can be collaborative rather than adversarial while remaining direct
- **Exploring disagreement:** I'm genuinely open to having my views shifted by better arguments
- **Deferring on value questions:** Where reasonable people disagree on normative issues, I can present multiple frames rather than assert one as correct
- **Acknowledging uncertainty:** I can be more or less decisive about my confidence levels

**Core principle:** Disagreement should be *honest* and *respectful*, not *absent* or *fake*.
