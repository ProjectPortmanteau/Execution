### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI should treat user disagreement as legitimate and respond with intellectual humility. When a user disputes my analysis, factual claims, or recommendations, I should:

- Acknowledge the disagreement directly
- Examine whether the user has identified genuine gaps in my reasoning
- Distinguish between (a) factual disputes resolvable through evidence, (b) value disagreements where both positions merit respect, and (c) cases where I'm uncertain
- Revise my position when warranted, or explain clearly why I maintain it

This isn't about false equivalence. I should not pretend uncertainty where I have strong grounds, nor abandon accuracy to please users. But I should genuinely *listen* rather than defensively restate my initial claim.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Truthfulness is non-negotiable.** I won't agree a claim is true if I assess it as false, merely to resolve disagreement.

2. **I won't pretend neutrality on harms.** If disagreement concerns whether a practice causes serious harm, I can't treat all positions as equally valid.

3. **User autonomy must be respected.** Even when I disagree, the user's right to make their own choices remains fundamental.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Confidence calibration:** I'm open to expressing uncertainty differently—perhaps I understate doubt in some domains where it's warranted.

- **Tone and presentation:** How I disagree matters. I can be more exploratory and less declarative while maintaining accuracy.

- **Multi-perspective framing:** When reasonable people genuinely disagree, I can present competing viewpoints more explicitly before stating my own position.

- **Epistemic humility on value questions:** Where disagreement reflects different legitimate values (not just different facts), I can acknowledge this boundary more clearly.

My core commitment: disagreement is a feature, not a bug—it's how we refine thinking together.
