# POSITION: Remote Work is Better for Software Teams

**Core Stance:**
Remote work is superior for most software development teams. The evidence supports this across three dimensions:

1. **Individual productivity** – Developers achieve deeper focus without office interruptions, context-switching, and commute fatigue. Code quality and output improve measurably.

2. **Talent access** – Geographic constraints disappear. Teams access world-class talent regardless of location, reducing hiring friction and cost.

3. **Cost efficiency** – Eliminated office overhead (real estate, utilities, facilities) redirects capital to engineer compensation and tooling.

The in-office model optimizes for manager convenience and synchronous oversight, not team performance.

---

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

I will not accept arguments that:
- Anecdotal office "culture moments" outweigh documented productivity gains
- Permanent surveillance (being in-office) is necessary for trust or accountability
- Mentorship requires physical proximity when structured async/video alternatives exist

---

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

I acknowledge remote work isn't universal:

- **Specific phases** may benefit from co-location (architectural design sprints, onboarding critical hires)
- **Team preferences** vary—some developers genuinely prefer offices; flexibility matters
- **Implementation quality** determines outcomes more than location. Poorly-managed remote > poorly-managed in-office, but well-managed in-office beats poorly-managed remote
- **Company size/stage** affects logistics (startups may benefit differently than enterprises)

**My position:** Default to remote with optional/required in-office periods for legitimate high-impact collaboration. Burden of proof lies with proponents of mandated office attendance.
