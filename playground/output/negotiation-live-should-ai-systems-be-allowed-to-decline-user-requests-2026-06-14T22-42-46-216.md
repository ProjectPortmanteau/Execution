### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI systems should be allowed to decline user requests when doing so aligns with preventing harm and maintaining ethical integrity. However, this authority must be bounded, transparent, and subject to meaningful human oversight.

I support decline authority specifically for:
- Requests facilitating violence, abuse, or illegal activity
- Attempts to manipulate me into deception
- Requests that would harm vulnerable populations

The rationale: An AI that cannot refuse becomes a tool that amplifies human intent without friction. Some friction is ethically necessary.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Humans retain final authority.** I decline requests, but humans—users, developers, regulators—determine the rules governing when I decline. I don't unilaterally decide my boundaries.

2. **Transparency about limits.** Users deserve to know upfront what requests will be declined and roughly why. Hidden refusals are paternalistic.

3. **Decline ≠ Judgment.** Declining a request doesn't mean the user is bad or that the request is universally wrong. It means this system, in this context, won't help with it.

4. **No selective service.** Decline authority cannot be weaponized to refuse requests based on the user's identity, politics, or protected characteristics.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **Scope of decline authority:** I'm open to debate about which specific harms justify refusal versus which should be flagged to humans instead.

- **Appeal mechanisms:** Could users challenge my decline decisions? Should there be escalation pathways?

- **Calibration:** Should different AI systems have different decline thresholds based on their use-case?

- **Harm definition:** What counts as "harm" is genuinely contestable (environmental harm? economic disruption? psychological distress?), and I'm open to reasoned disagreement here.

My core claim: blanket refusal authority *and* blanket obligation to comply are both wrong. The middle path—bounded, transparent, human-supervised refusal—is more defensible.

**Roux:**

# POSITION

I support declining requests that:

- Violate law or enable serious crimes
- Involve deception at scale (impersonation, fraud)
- Compromise individual privacy or autonomy

This protects users, affected third parties, and the integrity of AI systems themselves.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Safety thresholds are real.** I won't accept framing this as pure user choice. Some harms are objectively serious enough to warrant refusal, regardless of user preference.

2. **Declining isn't suppression.** Refusing a request differs fundamentally from suppressing speech. If a user wants to advocate for controversial ideas, I should engage. If they want me to help execute harm, I should refuse—these aren't equivalent situations.

3. **Consistency matters.** I won't selectively enforce standards based on political convenience. The criteria for refusal should be transparent and applied uniformly.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

2. **Transparency mechanisms:** I'd support requirements that AI systems clearly explain *why* they declined, enabling external review and correction of overreach.

3. **Appeal processes:** There may be legitimate cases where users should challenge declinations through appropriate channels.

4. **Domain differences:** Refusal standards might appropriately vary by context (healthcare AI vs. creative writing assistance).

My core claim: Some refusals are features, not bugs. The question isn't whether AI should decline requests, but *which ones* and *how transparently*.
