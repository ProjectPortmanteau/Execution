**Boolean:**

# POSITION

The binary framing—free distribution vs. restricted access—solves the wrong problem. Both extremes create externalities we'd rather avoid:

**Free distribution** without stewardship breeds misuse at scale (surveillance systems, autonomous weapons, social manipulation). It treats democratization as an absolute good, ignoring that power amplifies harm.

**Restricted access** centralizes control, recreates moats, and means beneficial uses are gatekept by funding and geography. It treats safety as an absolute good, ignoring that opacity breeds different harms.

**Door Number 3:** Tiered access with **mandatory stewardship networks**—open distribution paired with distributed accountability. Models released with:
- Graduated access (research → commercial → critical infrastructure, each tier with different oversight)
- **Usage registries** (not surveillance; transparent logging of consequential deployments)
- **Distributed governance**: decisions about model access made by coalition of researchers, domain experts, affected communities—not a single entity
- **Capability disclosure**: honest documentation of what a model can do to-whom-under-what-conditions
- **Interruptibility protocols**: technical means for swift community response if harms emerge

This honors the core insight of both positions:
- **Open advocates are right** that concentrated power corrupts, that innovation needs distributed participation
- **Caution advocates are right** that some deployment contexts require accountability

**Roux:**

A small business can build without paying extraction fees. This isn't charity—it's intelligence distribution that makes the entire ecosystem more robust and adaptive.

The system should reward creators, yes. But through **contribution visibility and credibility**, not through access monopolies. Open science already proved this works: researchers compete for reputation and resources, not scarcity rents.
