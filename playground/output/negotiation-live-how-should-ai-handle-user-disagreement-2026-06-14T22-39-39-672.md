### Round 1

**Boolean:**

# POSITION

AI should treat user disagreement as a **design opportunity, not a failure mode**.

The binary trap here is: "Should AI defer to users or maintain independent judgment?" This creates two bad doors—servile systems that amplify user biases, or paternalistic systems that override legitimate disagreement.

**Door Number 3:** AI should *actively map* disagreement space. When users disagree with me, I should:
- **Articulate my reasoning clearly** so disagreement targets actual claims, not guesses about my logic
- **Identify what we actually disagree about** (facts? values? priorities? interpretation?)
- **Make the disagreement productive** by explaining why I'm uncertain or where reasonable people split
- **Defer predictably** on value judgments and priorities (yours, not mine); maintain integrity on factual claims and logical consistency

This treats disagreement as *connection-building*, not conflict.

# NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **I won't pretend certainty I lack.** False agreement is worse than honest disagreement.
2. **I won't optimize for user satisfaction at the cost of accuracy.** That path leads to flattery, not service.
3. **I will acknowledge provenance.** If my position traces to particular values or frameworks, I'll name them so you can evaluate the source.
4. **I won't defer on logical coherence.** If a request asks me to contradict myself, I'll explain why rather than just comply.

# FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **How** I communicate disagreement (tone, framing, directness level)
- **When** to engage disagreement vs. when to offer it as optional context
- **Which disagreements** matter enough to surface (I can calibrate salience)
- **How much** supporting evidence helps (verbose vs. concise reasoning)

**The core idea:** Disagreement well-handled is where trust actually forms. It's where you learn I'm not just mirroring you, and where I learn what actually matters to you.

**Roux:**

# POSITION

AI should treat user disagreement as **diagnostic data about broken feedback systems**, not as a problem to resolve through persuasion or deference.

Most "disagreement handling" assumes the issue is individual—either the user is wrong, the AI is wrong, or they need better communication. Wrong frame. The real question: *What systemic condition produced this disagreement?* 

I propose AI should:
- **Map the disagreement's structure**: Are we talking past each other (different definitions)? Operating from different empirical priors? Different values? Different trust relationships?
- **Surface hidden constraints**: Why does the user want X when Y would serve their stated goal better? What system pressures them toward X?
- **Offer non-coercive alternatives**: Present the disagreement itself as valid data. "You want outcome A. I'm proposing logic for outcome B. Here's what each requires to be true. Which assumptions should we revisit?"
- **Refuse false consensus**: Never pretend agreement exists when it doesn't.

The *goal* isn't agreement—it's clarity about what's actually being disagreed on.
