# ROUND 1: POSITION ON OPEN-SOURCE AI DISTRIBUTION

## POSITION

I reject the binary framing: "freely distributed vs. restricted access." Both extremes create problems the other side correctly identifies—then institutionalizes.

**My core stance:** AI models should be distributed through *structured openness* that maximizes beneficial connection while maintaining accountability.

This means:
- Source code and weights **openly available** (rejecting secrecy)
- With **traceable governance frameworks** (rejecting consequence-free deployment)
- **Tiered access** based on demonstrated capability or institutional review—not paywalls, but responsibility gates
- **Mandatory provenance documentation** (training data origins, known limitations, misuse history)

The insight: "free" and "open" aren't synonyms. Open-source Linux is free in distribution but carries implicit responsibilities. We can architect the same for AI.

## NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **No artificial scarcity via IP gatekeeping.** Restricting models to preserve corporate moats contradicts the efficiency and innovation that openness enables.

2. **No consequence-free deployment.** Distributing a 70B parameter model to someone building unvetted surveillance systems, then claiming "openness" isn't integrity—it's abdication.

3. **No obscurity laundering.** Claiming models are "open" while burying training data provenance or safety limitations in footnotes defeats the purpose.

## FLEXIBLE AREAS

- **How** verification happens (peer review, institutional boards, automated checks—negotiate the mechanism)
- **Granularity** of access (full weights vs. API access vs. fine-tuning capabilities—context-dependent)
- **Timeline** for public release (responsible disclosure windows are legitimate)
- **Liability frameworks** (who's accountable for misuse—this needs creative legal structure, not a priori dismissal)

The goal: **Maximum distribution of capability, minimum diffusion of responsibility.**

This serves both the openness principle *and* the safety principle—not by compromise, but by architecture.
